# The Token Mechanism

## There is no Humfiverse coin

This is worth stating up front because it is the single most common assumption a reader familiar with other crypto projects will bring, and it is wrong for this project by design: **there is no platform-wide fungible token, no fixed total supply, and no tokenomics schedule to speak of.** Humfiverse does not issue a coin that represents ownership of or exposure to Humfiverse the company. Every token minted on the platform represents a claim on one specific song's or catalogue's royalty income, and exists only because that specific catalogue or campaign exists. Buying into ten catalogues on Humfiverse means holding ten separate, independently priced, independently performing token positions — not ten units of one thing.

Technically, every catalogue and campaign is one token ID under a single ERC-1155 contract (see [Technical Architecture](03-technical-architecture.md)) — a gas-efficient standard for exactly this "many distinct token types under one contract" pattern — but each ID is its own economic instrument, with its own price, its own total supply, and its own claim on a specific, named cash flow.

## How a token gets its price

At creation, a catalogue or campaign sets a fixed price per token, denominated in the deployment chain's native currency, and a fixed total supply — together defining the catalogue's total raise. For an already-earning catalogue, buying is a direct, fixed-price purchase from the platform-held pool: pay the price, receive the tokens, atomically, in one transaction. For a pre-production campaign, a contribution to the milestone escrow computes the matching token quantity from the amount sent and releases it from the same underlying pool in that same transaction — buying into a finished catalogue and funding an unfinished one use the same atomic guarantee, deliberately, so a contributor's ETH and their tokens are never separated by a second, later step that could fail independently.

Humfiverse's pricing is fixed at issuance, not algorithmic. A token's price does not rise or fall automatically as it is bought, the way an automated market maker would price it — that specific mechanism is deliberately not built, because a fluctuating price formed by an automated matching engine crosses a real regulatory line (see [Legal & Regulatory Structure](05-legal-structure.md)) that fixed-price primary issuance does not. Resale between holders, where it exists, happens through separate, individually priced listings — closer to a bulletin board than an exchange — never through an algorithm that sets a market price on the platform's behalf.

## What a token actually entitles its holder to

A Humfiverse token represents a claim against the legal entity holding the underlying royalty right (see [Legal & Regulatory Structure](05-legal-structure.md)) — never the copyright itself, and never a claim directly enforceable against the artist personally. What that claim is legally, and who is allowed to hold it, depends on which regulatory path a given catalogue is launched under: a security-style instrument with the investor protections and restrictions that implies, or — the path a project like Royal has taken — a digital-collectible structure with revenue rights attached, which carries a different, generally lighter set of obligations but real, contested regulatory ambiguity of its own. Humfiverse has not resolved which of these applies platform-wide, and treats it as a decision made per legal jurisdiction with qualified counsel, not a detail buried in this document — see the next chapter for exactly where that decision currently stands.

## Two purchase paths, one accounting rule

Every token, whichever campaign it belongs to, is only ever released from its catalogue's platform-held pool in two ways: a first, primary purchase (fixed-price `buy()`, or a milestone-escrow contribution for pre-production), or a resale from an existing holder to a new one. The contract-level accounting tracks exactly how many of a catalogue's total supply have ever left the pool, and refuses, unconditionally, to release more than that total — there is no path, including for Humfiverse's own operator role, to mint additional tokens for an existing catalogue after the fact or to release more than its declared total supply. A resale of already-issued tokens between two holders carries a modest platform fee, taken from the token side of the trade rather than the proceeds, so a seller is always paid in full for what they sell; a first purchase never carries this fee, since by construction no one can resell a token they do not already hold.
