# Humfiverse

### Fractional ownership of music royalty income, on-chain

> **This is a concept and pilot-stage document, not an offering.** Humfiverse has not raised money from investors and is not licensed by any financial regulator. Nothing here is investment advice. The smart contracts described in this paper run on Ethereum Sepolia — a **test network only.** No real money moves through them today. See [Current Status & Disclaimer](09-status-disclaimer.md) before reading anything else here as a claim about what exists versus what's planned.

## The idea, in short

A song generates royalties every time it's streamed, licensed, or performed. That income is real and recurring — but it's normally locked away with labels and publishers, out of reach for the artist's own fans and for ordinary investors. Humfiverse turns *a share of a song's royalty income* into a token: something that can be split into small pieces, bought, and held.

Two ways to do this:

1. **Catalogue tokenization** — tokenize a song that's already out and already earning. You're buying a share of a known, ongoing income stream.
2. **Pre-production financing** — fund an unfinished track (studio time, musicians, mixing) in exchange for a share of what it earns once it's released. You're funding something that doesn't exist yet.

Both sit on the same foundation: a legal entity holds the real royalty right, a token represents a claim on it, and a smart contract handles what can honestly be automated — custody of funds, paying out on schedule, releasing money only when production milestones are actually confirmed — without pretending to automate the things that genuinely need a human and a regulator: proving who owns a royalty right, or deciding who's allowed to buy this.

## Why this document is unusually blunt

Most whitepapers describe a token and an infrastructure that don't exist yet. This one is the opposite: the technology — two smart contracts, deployed, tested, and verified on a public testnet, running a real working app — is ahead of the legal and business side, which is still genuinely open on several big questions. Where that's true, this document says so directly, chapter by chapter, instead of writing around it. A project whose entire pitch is "verifiable, on-chain, checkable by anyone" should hold its own whitepaper to that same standard.
